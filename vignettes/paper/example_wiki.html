Web traffic: five-day conditional-batch contribution penalties

Data and week-like batching

Daily English Wikipedia pageviews for 30 articles span 2022-01-01 through 2024-12-31 in the bundled inst/extdata/wiki_traffic.csv. Covariates are the current day’s article views, in thousands; the response is the next day’s aggregate count. The aggregate is omitted from the predictors because it is the sum of the article series. The final covariate row is dropped because its next-day target is unavailable.

Models use one-year training windows refit monthly. Evaluation covariates span 2023-01-01 through 2024-12-30, with targets through 2024-12-31. We retain walk = 5 for computational efficiency as a five-day, week-like conditional batch. Slices contain five consecutive daily rows (including weekends), restart each month, and may end with a shorter slice.

All covariates in the slice enter the penalty together. Responses in that slice do not enter fitting. The five-row procedure is therefore conditional on the batch’s covariates, not a five-day-ahead forecast issued using only information known on its first day. It is also not a weekly aggregate target: scoring remains at the daily next-day-count level. A deployment that does not know the batch covariates must supply predictor scenarios or use walk = 1. This distinction lets us retain the faster experiment without overstating its forecast-origin information set.

Estimation

We use the Poisson log-link loss as a working conditional-mean objective, not a claim of a calibrated Poisson count distribution. lambda = 0 isolates the contribution penalty; alpha is immaterial. The feature-specific ceiling is three times the absolute training univariate Poisson slope. This differs from the finance correlation rule. The gamma grid is fixed at c(10, 100, 1000, 10000, 100000).

The ordinary capnet fit with gamma zero supplies the uncapped baseline. Post-fit capping clips its slope contributions on the log-mean scale, retains the intercept, and exponentiates to get predicted counts. Batches are evaluated serially in this reproduction; changing independent execution order is not a statistical change to the experiment.

Scores and diagnostics

The score is squared-error out-of-sample R-squared, not a deviance-based pseudo-R-squared. calc_oos_r2 compares summed squared errors to an expanding mean of preceding evaluation responses and drops the first observation.

The complete grid is a sensitivity analysis. The plotted gamma is the one with the largest full-window score, chosen retrospectively. It is not a separately validated deployment choice, and its performance is not an unbiased estimate of a tuning procedure’s future performance.

The figure compares each method’s maximum absolute feature contribution on one shared logarithmic vertical axis. The axis compresses the isolated 2023 uncapped spike while keeping the smaller daily values visible. Its tick labels give contribution magnitudes on the original log-mean scale.

Rolling scores use 180 scored observations, consistent with the original vignette. Each window is summed directly to avoid cumulative-subtraction cancellation after a very large forecast error leaves the window. The pooled scores above and the 2024-only comparison below summarize predictive accuracy. The contribution figure computes the nonnegative maximum absolute contribution separately for each model and row, rather than tracking a reference model’s selected feature or plotting signed values.
