Market timing: reproducible contribution-penalty comparison

Protocol

The bundled inst/extdata/data.csv contains daily market observations through 2026-02-13. We use the eleven feature columns listed below, without constructing additional predictors. Training windows are 25 calendar years, refit monthly; the evaluation starts in January 2020. Complete training cases are used. The historical training window stays fixed as the cap penalty walks over individual rows in the following month (walk = 1).

At each refit, cv.glmnet selects lambda.min with alpha = 0.5, ten random folds, and standardization. Monthly seeds start at 42 and increase by one. This lambda is shared across methods and gamma values. Random inner folds are retained from the original vignette; the outer evaluation remains chronological. This design is not forward-chaining inner validation.

Ceilings use the original helper rule \(L_j=7|\mathrm{cor}(X_j,y)|/350\) on the complete training cases. This is a scale-dependent correlation heuristic, not a univariate slope rule. The ordinary capnet fit with gamma = 0 supplies the uncapped model; post-fit capping clips its contributions and retains its intercept. The walk-forward grid is c(0.1, 1, 10, 100).

The data are taken as supplied by the competition. Their proprietary feature release times cannot be independently verified from this file; no portfolio return or investment-risk guarantee is inferred from these forecast results.

Forecast scores

calc_oos_r2 reports one pooled squared-error score, \(1-\sum(y_t-\hat y_t)^2/\sum(y_t-\bar y_{t-1})^2\). The benchmark averages preceding evaluation-period responses, not initial training responses, and omits the first evaluation observation. This is not an average of monthly R-squared values.

Runtime protocol

Runtime uses three serial repetitions of the entire backtest for each of glmnet, capnet, and walk_capnet, including monthly CV and output assembly. The two contribution-penalized methods both use gamma 0.1. The same monthly seeds and lambda-search defaults are used in every repetition. The initial package installation/loading is excluded. Requested BLAS/OpenMP thread limits are one, and no experiment-level parallelism is used for these timings. Reported numbers describe this machine and run, not portable solver rankings.
